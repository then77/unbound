title: Unbound for users
description: Understand what happens when an app asks you to sign in through Unbound and who controls each part of the experience.
---

Unbound lets an app connect to an external sign-in provider without requiring you to create a separate Unbound account.

## What happens during sign-in

When an app uses Unbound:

1. The app sends you to an Unbound authorization request.
2. You check the app origin and the information it requests.
3. You choose an available provider, currently Google, GitHub, or Discord depending on the deployment.
4. You authenticate with that provider if needed.
5. You approve or deny the app’s request.
6. After approval, Unbound returns you to the app so it can complete sign-in.

You can deny a request that is unexpected or asks for information you do not want to share.

## Who controls what

| Party | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| **Your provider** | Manages your provider account, credentials, provider login, and its own account and privacy rules. |
| **Unbound** | Brokers the sign-in, shows the app origin and requested scopes, and issues the app a short-lived identity token after approval. |
| **The app** | Decides whether you can access the app, which roles or permissions you have, and how it stores and uses approved data. |

Unbound authenticates identity for the app. It does not make the app trustworthy or control what the app does after it receives approved information.

## Your app-scoped Unbound ID

The app receives a pairwise Unbound ID derived for that app origin. A different app origin receives a different ID for the same provider identity.

This design reduces direct cross-app correlation through one shared Unbound identifier. It does not guarantee anonymity or make tracking and correlation impossible; apps and providers can have other information and operate under their own policies.

## What Unbound is not

Unbound is not:

- a separate user account you can recover;
- the app’s permission, role, or moderation system;
- a guarantee that an app is legitimate or safe;
- a replacement for managing your provider account with the provider; or
- a way to revoke data an app has already received.
